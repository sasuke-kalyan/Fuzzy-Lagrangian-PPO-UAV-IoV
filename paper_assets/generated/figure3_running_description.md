### Proposed System Architecture

**Figure 3. Proposed Fuzzy LP-PPO system architecture for reliable UAV selection in a dynamic UAV-assisted IoV environment.**

Figure 3 presents the complete architecture of the proposed Fuzzy LP-PPO system, including the online communication-decision flow and the supporting training, constraint-adaptation, and recording processes. The dynamic UAV–IoV environment in Fig. 3(A) represents the urban-canyon, suburban-crossroads, and emergency-response scenarios. It contains eight physical candidate UAVs and a representative active vehicle, because one active vehicle is served during each selection decision. Vehicle mobility, UAV positions, scenario conditions, and residual UAV energy form the physical state that changes between consecutive decisions.

For every candidate vehicle–UAV pair, the link-measurement engine in Fig. 3(B) calculates the three-dimensional communication distance, signal quality, delay, packet delivery ratio (PDR), and residual UAV energy. These measurements support two parallel information paths. First, the observation constructor in Fig. 3(C) produces the normalized 34-dimensional state supplied to PPO. This state contains two normalized vehicle-position features and four features for each of the eight UAVs: relative x-position, relative y-position, signal quality, and normalized residual energy. Delay, PDR, fuzzy priority, and Lagrangian multipliers are used elsewhere in the decision process and are not included directly in the 34-dimensional PPO observation.

The candidate-assessment subsystem in Fig. 3(D) converts the measured communication conditions into a ranked list of usable UAVs. The hard feasibility screen in Fig. 3(D1) retains candidates satisfying the distance, delay, signal, and normalized-energy thresholds. If no UAV satisfies all four conditions, the complete UAV set is retained to prevent an empty action set. The clipped fuzzy-risk module in Fig. 3(D2) then calculates continuous delay, PDR, signal, and energy risks, together with the emergency-scenario boost when applicable. Finally, the ranking module in Fig. 3(D3) orders the retained candidates using the implemented composite score, which combines signal quality, PDR, delay, normalized energy, fuzzy priority, and a distance penalty.

The PPO actor–critic in Fig. 3(E) receives the 34-dimensional observation independently of the candidate-ranking path. Its actor produces a probability distribution over eight ranked-action positions, while its critic estimates the state value. During training, an action is sampled from the policy distribution; during online evaluation, the highest-probability rank is used. The PPO rank index and the ranked UAV list meet in the rank-to-UAV mapping component shown in Fig. 3(F). This component maps the selected ordinal position to the corresponding physical UAV \(u^*\), ensuring that a PPO action represents a position in the current ranked list rather than a permanent UAV identity.

After mapping, the communication-execution component in Fig. 3(G) establishes the vehicle-to-UAV link with \(u^*\) and applies the communication-energy drain. The QoS and reward component in Fig. 3(H) records the selected-link signal, delay, PDR, and energy values and calculates the task reward, QoS contribution, fuzzy-priority contribution, and adaptive constraint penalty. The pre-drain energy is used during the current reward calculation, while the post-drain remaining energy is stored as the updated environment value. The decision-output component in Fig. 3(I) exposes the requested rank, effective UAV, action-mask information, link measurements, remaining energy, reward, and multiplier values. It also updates vehicle mobility, selected-UAV energy, and the decision counter to construct the next environment state.

The persistent multiplier controller in Fig. 3(J) monitors delay, PDR, energy, and signal violations after every executed decision. A multiplier increases when its corresponding constraint is violated, remains unchanged when the constraint is satisfied, persists across episode resets, and is clipped to the interval \([0,10]\). The resulting adaptive penalty is applied to subsequent reward calculations in Fig. 3(H). Thus, repeatedly violated constraints receive progressively greater importance without placing the multiplier values inside the PPO observation.

The scenario-specific training process in Fig. 3(K) performs the fuzzy-guided warm start and PPO optimization. Each scenario uses 800 warm-start states, six behavior-cloning epochs, 100 training episodes of 50 decisions, 200-step rollouts, generalized advantage estimation, and ten PPO update epochs. A separate trained actor–critic policy is saved for each scenario. Finally, the logging and evaluation interface in Fig. 3(L) stores episode rewards, timing information, selected-UAV decisions, QoS measurements, energy values, and multipliers. These outputs support the subsequent performance graphs, tables, baseline comparisons, constraint analysis, and publication reporting.
